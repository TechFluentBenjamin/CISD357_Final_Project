# Data Analays Report

The authors, Tristen Garcia and Benjamin Hays, were asked to develop three charts that tells stories about the data while aligned with the preapproved research questions. These questions explored connections between two variables - salary to RBIs, and RBIs to the player's month born. Three charts were developed using Matplotlib and pandas, and will be discussed indepdently.

## Player Salary to RBI

The authors investigated if the amount of money a player makes is aligned with the amount of RBIs a player is able to make who have sinced retired from Major League Baseball in 2024. A scatter chart was developed.

![Chart 1: Player Salary to RBI](chart1.png)
*Figure 1. Player Salary to RBI*

The authors decided a scatterchart with a trendline would best communicate if there is a relationship between RBIs and salary. The highest paid player had zero RBIs. Additionally a p-value of over 0.05 exist, showing no relationship between salary and RBIs. 

Additionally, there is an R Squred value of about 0. This shows there is no coorrolation between salary and RBI. Clearly, there is another varaible that affects the salary a player receives other then an RBI.

## Player Birthmonth to RBI

The authors investigated if the month the retired player was born aligns with the amount of RBIs a player is able to make who have sinced retired from the MLB in 2024. A bar chart was developed. 

![Chat2: Player Birth Month to RBI](chart2.png)
*Figure 2. Player Birth Month to RBI*

The authors decided a bar chart would best communicate this information. The X axis had the birth month, and the Y axis had the total RBIs of all players who were born within that month. The lowest RBI month was May, with a total RBIs was about 135,000. The highest RBI was October, with a total RBIs of about 172,000. The difference is about 25%. Per the decided cutoff, there is a coorolation between birth month and RBIs hit. Other factors may influence this, including the average birth month of the global population, or related factors.
